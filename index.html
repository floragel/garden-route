South Africa Honestly

The Traveler's Truth

The Past: A "Forbidden Garden"

South Africa’s beauty has a complex past. During Apartheid (1948–1994), non-white citizens were legally excluded from many of the beaches and resorts you will visit. This history created a massive economic gap that remains visible today.

Why Your Visit Matters

When you visit respectfully, your tourism dollars directly support the "Rainbow Nation's" growth. Every local guide you hire and every craft you buy helps bridge the gap created by the past.

How to Behave Respectfully

  • Ask before filming: Especially in rural areas; respect the privacy of locals.
  • Tip generously: 10-15% is the standard. It is a vital part of the service staff’s income.
  • Engage with curiosity: Don't shy away from history. Most SA citizens are proud to share their story of resilience.
  • Water awareness: South Africa is a water-scarce country; keep showers short.

The Garden Route: Then vs. Now

Then (Apartheid Era): Under the Separate Amenities Act, the best beaches, hiking trails, and lookouts along the Garden Route were reserved for "Whites Only." Black and Coloured South Africans were barred from the very landscapes they called home.

Now: Every beach and national park is legally open to everyone. You will see a true "Rainbow Nation" enjoying the braais (BBQs) and lagoons together. However, economic inequality remains a barrier; while the law changed, affording these luxury experiences is still a work in progress.

    Hike the dramatic cliffs of the Robberg Peninsula and look down upon great white sharks and Cape fur seals.

    Oudtshoorn
